# Model for the carpenter

Read [fabrication modeling](../samaya-revit/references/fabrication-modeling.md) and [joinery practice](../samaya-revit/references/joinery-practice.md) for stock, finish, and build-up rules.

1. Start with the site opening, finished dimensions, and confirmed material. Break the unit into boards, frames, backs, fillers, plinths, and bought hardware as applicable. Model the layers and edge treatment that control finished size. Use stock the shop can buy. Build up a thick edge from the specified layers.
2. Lay out repeat modules from one type when the design and fit allow it. Use fillers to take up site variation. Split the assembly where the carpenter needs to make, carry, or install separate units. Give those units their own marks. Check that they fit through the delivery route.
3. Model the actual joint, rebate, setback, scribe gap, or shadow gap where the carpenter needs it. Show where cleats, battens, or hardware attach. Work through which panel goes in first and which panel comes out for service. For a powered unit, check the cable path, driver access, and fit of the LED channel.
4. Render a cut section through the joint and confirm the cut faces and material layers are visible. Check marks, part and overall dimensions, the fit against surveyed structure, and the approved finish on each part. Detail anything the carpenter cannot read from the section. Use the CNC skill when a part needs a machine-cut profile.

**Shelf example.** When three cabinets are made and fitted separately, place three individually marked instances with their own backs. Check both joints, the centre setback, the outer corner curves, and clearance to the structure. A shelf divided into three segments inside one family cannot represent three separately handled cabinets.
