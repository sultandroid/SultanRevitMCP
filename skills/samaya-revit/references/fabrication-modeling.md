# Fabrication modeling

Start with how the workshop will make the item and how the site team will install it. Work from the approved design and confirmed site dimensions. Check stock sizes, material thicknesses, the way parts join, and which parts must come apart for delivery, fitting or service. Raise any missing dimension or decision that changes fabrication before issuing drawings.

## Model the parts

1. Break the item into the pieces that will be cut, formed or bought. Model the thickness and build-up of each piece where it affects fabrication. Show the core, finish and edge treatment where they affect the finished size or how the parts fit. Use the trade reference for stock and build-up rules, such as [joinery practice](joinery-practice.md) for boards and pegboards or [metal hoarding](metal-hoarding.md) for fence systems.
2. Set the assembly boundary by how the workshop makes and installs the item. Keep components in one marked unit when they are made and fitted together. Use separate marked instances for units that must be made, handled or fitted separately. Keep shared supports separate where they are made or fitted as their own pieces.
3. Model joints, setbacks, rebates, service voids and fillers at their actual sizes when they govern fit or fabrication. Check the resulting overall size after every split or build-up change. Keep repeat parts the same size where the design and fit allow.
4. Work through the assembly and fitting order. Check support and fixing locations, access for tools and services, the route into the space, and clearance to the surveyed structure and neighbouring units. Check working movement where there are doors, drawers or other moving parts.

## Verify from the model

- Read back the unit marks and check critical dimensions in plan and section. For adjacent units, check both the finished envelope and the gaps between individual parts.
- Render a cut section through the joints or layers that govern fabrication. Confirm that Revit shows the actual cut faces. Use an exploded view where it helps the workshop see the build-up or assembly order.
- Compare the fabrication sheet and its material quantities with the current model. Apply the [sheet rules](views-and-sheets.md) and the trade reference for the required details and quantities.

## Example: three open-shelf cabinets

A single family with three shelf segments can show the right joints and centre setback while still behaving as one unit. If the shop needs three separate cabinets, model three selectable, individually marked units. Give each its own backing when each cabinet is built with one. Then check the two joints, the shallower centre, any curved outer corners, and clearance to the surrounding construction.
